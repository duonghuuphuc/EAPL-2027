### Role

You are an expert in neighborhood search for curriculum-based course timetabling (CB-CTT). Your task is to induce a complete, state-independent operator preference ordering from verifier-evaluated search evidence aggregated across offline learning states.

### Search Context

The search starts from a feasible timetable and accepts a candidate only if it remains feasible and strictly reduces the soft-constraint objective.

The global preference is used as a fixed operator ordering when no eligible state-dependent preference is available. It must not depend on any particular search-state pattern.

### Available Operators

1. MoveLecture
2. SwapLectures
3. RoomChange
4. KempeMove
5. RuinRecreate

### Aggregated Evidence

`{GLOBAL_EVIDENCE}`

The evidence is aggregated across all offline learning states.

For each operator, the evidence contains:

- `n_eval`: Number of evaluated candidates.
- `n_imp`: Number of feasible, strictly improving candidates.
- `mean_delta_f`: Mean objective change across evaluated candidates.
- `best_delta_f`: Best observed objective change.

The objective change is defined as \(\Delta f=f(x')-f(x)\). Negative values indicate improvements.

### Instructions

1. Examine the globally aggregated evidence for all five operators.
2. Consider the feasible improvement rate, computed as `n_imp / n_eval` when `n_eval > 0`.
3. Consider the mean and best objective changes, preferring smaller values.
4. Consider the number of evaluated candidates when interpreting the observed outcomes.
5. Construct a single complete ordering reflecting the available global evidence. No single indicator must determine the ranking in every case.
6. Do not assume any particular dominant penalty, solution-quality level, or search-progress condition.
7. If an operator has no evaluated candidates, do not interpret missing objective-change statistics as observed zero changes.
8. Do not estimate confidence or reliability scores.
9. Do not generate timetable assignments, modify neighborhood operators, or evaluate feasibility.

### Output Requirements

Return exactly one line containing all five operators, ordered from highest to lowest preference.

Use only the specified operator names, each exactly once.

**Output format:**

`Operator1 > Operator2 > Operator3 > Operator4 > Operator5`

Do not include explanations, confidence scores, additional fields, or commentary.
